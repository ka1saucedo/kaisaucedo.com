The problem

A local service business competing for the same customers as every other shop on the island, with a budget too small to absorb waste. Reach alone is worthless here. A view from outside the service radius costs the same as one from a customer three miles away, and it's worth nothing.

The approach

Two campaigns, each with one job. Both were targeted to the area he can actually drive to.

  1. Reel 1. A short-form video built to hold attention and send people to the booking page. The hook and the pacing carry the cost per click, not the bid.
  2. Summer Brake Special. An offer-led ad built to start conversations in Messenger, for people who would rather ask a question than fill out a form.

The reel had 9,970 three-second views. About 20% of those viewers made it three-quarters of the way through, and 1,360 watched to the very end.

The outcome

4 jobs booked at $400 each, $1,600 in revenue. That's about 8.5x the reel's $188 in spend, and about 5.9x across everything spent in June.

The two campaigns did different jobs well. The reel was the cheap way to get people to the booking page, at $0.21 a click. The brake special was the cheap way to start a conversation, at $4.75 each versus $23.44 from the reel. Next time, I'd run them together on purpose: the reel for reach and traffic, the offer for people ready to talk.
